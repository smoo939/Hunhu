OMISA Province and the Zimbabwe Mission

MissionThe Oblates of Mary Immaculate Southern Africa (OMISA) Province represents a deliberate and strategic consolidation of Oblate missionary work across the region, and its stewardship of the Zimbabwe Mission is a clear expression of that purpose. Formed in 2017 through the amalgamation of the Northern, Natal, and Central Provinces of South Africa, OMISA brought together decades of experience, personnel, and institutional capacity to strengthen governance, support vocations, and expand ministries across Southern Africa. Taking responsibility for the Zimbabwe Mission—originally established in 1983—allowed the new Province to integrate Zimbabwean ministry into a broader regional framework, ensuring more consistent formation, administrative support, and pastoral outreach.

Origins of the Zimbabwe Mission

MissionThe Zimbabwe Mission began in 1983 at the invitation of local bishops and through the initiative of Fr. Paddy Hogan OMI, then General Councillor for Africa. Responding especially to Bishop Henry Karlen of Bulawayo, the Oblates sent their first missionaries—Fr. Bob de Sylva in April 1983 and Fr. Paul Horrocks in June 1983—to Our Lady of Lourdes Parish, Kumalo, in Bulawayo. They arrived in a region still recovering from war and facing a severe shortage of priests and pastoral resources. From the outset the mission prioritized grassroots pastoral work: establishing small Christian communities, accompanying parish life, and laying the foundations for local leadership.

Building Local Vocations and Formations

A central aim of the early years (1983–1995) was to foster indigenous vocations and sustainable local ministry. The opening of a pre-novitiate in 1992 marked a concrete step toward that goal, creating a pathway for Zimbabwean men to discern and prepare for Oblate life. Between 1996 and 2001 the Natal Province assumed full responsibility for the mission, strengthening formation programs and expanding the pre-novitiate to better support candidates. These investments in formation bore fruit over time, culminating in a milestone in 2006 when Fr. Charles Rensburg OMI became the first Zimbabwean-born Oblate priest—an event that signaled a maturing local church and the emergence of homegrown leadership.
